Health

Overnight recovery, sleep and daily strain

Recovery

180d

A same-morning readiness score, not a running "how tired am I" tally — it's how well your body bounced back overnight, from this morning's HRV, resting HR, and sleep vs. your own 30-day baseline (40%/30%/30% weighting). Approximate, not Whoop's algorithm. 0-100: ≥67 (green) is high — you're reading better than your own baseline, there's capacity to take on a normal or hard day. 34-66 (amber) is moderate — nothing's off, but there's no extra in the tank either. <34 (red) is low — one or more inputs are meaningfully worse than baseline, favor an easy or rest day, especially if it's been trending down for several days rather than a single dip.

Strain

180d

The training stress you actually put on your body that day — not a feeling of tiredness. It's a rough Whoop-Strain-style 0–21 score derived from that day's total Garmin/intervals.icu training load: a hard workout scores high strain regardless of how tired you subjectively felt doing it. Not continuous heart rate, which this repo doesn't have access to, so a true rest day shows 0 here (unlike real Whoop's small non-zero baseline). Hevy strength sessions don't carry a comparable load score yet, so lifting-only days currently show 0 too — a gap, not a sign nothing happened. 0-21: ≥14 (red) is a hard day's worth of load, 7-13 (amber) is moderate, <7 (grey) is easy or rest. Read it as spending against Recovery's balance: Recovery is how much capacity you had going into the day, Strain is how much of it you spent — high strain is expected and fine when Recovery was high; the combination worth acting on is high strain stacked on top of Recovery that was already amber or red.

Sleep score

180d

Garmin's 0–100 sleep quality score, combining duration, sleep stages, and restlessness overnight.

Sleep stages

hours, 60d

Time in each sleep stage per night — the same breakdown Whoop/Oura lead with. Deep and REM are generally the stages that matter most for physical and mental recovery, respectively.

HRV

180d

Heart rate variability — the variation in time between heartbeats, measured overnight. Higher generally means a more relaxed, recovered nervous system.

Resting HR

180d

Heart rate at rest, typically measured overnight. Lower usually reflects better cardiovascular fitness and recovery.

Respiration

breaths/min, 180d

Average overnight breathing rate. A rising trend can precede illness or heavy fatigue before other markers catch up — the same reasoning Whoop/Oura use it for.

Blood oxygen

SpO2 %, 180d

Overnight blood oxygen saturation. Sparse by nature — Garmin only records it on nights pulse-ox tracking happens to run.
